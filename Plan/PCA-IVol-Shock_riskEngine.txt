Objective B: implement constrained SSVIUse the Surface SVI parameterization.Refer to Gatheral and Jacquier, *Arbitrage-free SVI volatility surfaces*, Quantitative Finance, 2014:https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.0646https://doi.org/10.1080/14697688.2013.819986Verify the mathematical conditions directly against the paper and explain which conditions are necessary, sufficient, or conservative. Do not copy formulas from secondary sources without verification.### 6.1 SSVI total-variance formulaImplement:`w(k, theta) = theta/2 * (1 + rho*phi(theta)*k + sqrt((phi(theta)*k + rho)**2 + 1 - rho**2))`with:- `theta > 0`- `-1 < rho < 1`- `phi(theta) > 0`- `sigma(k,T) = sqrt(w(k,theta(T)) / T)`Here `theta(T)` is ATM total variance, not implied volatility. At `k = 0`, the formula must satisfy `w(0,theta) = theta`.Implement well-tested vectorized NumPy functions for total variance, implied volatility, and any relevant derivatives.Handle numerical edge cases near zero variance without generating NaNs, complex numbers, or invalid square roots.### 6.2 Initial choice of smile-shape functionFor the first implementation, use the restricted power-law family:`phi(theta) = eta / (theta**gamma * (1 + theta)**(1 - gamma))`with:- `eta > 0`- `0 < gamma < 0.5`, allowing boundary limits only when explicitly handled.- `-1 < rho < 1`.Restricting gamma to this range is an intentional conservative design choice for the first implementation. It simplifies the global admissibility conditions for this functional form. Document the limitation rather than silently expanding the allowed range.### 6.3 Enforce parameter constraints after every simulated shockSimulate unconstrained latent parameters and transform them into admissible SSVI parameters.A suitable construction is:`rho = (1 - eps_rho) * tanh(u_rho)``gamma = 0.5 * sigmoid(u_gamma)``eta_max(rho) = 2 / (1 + abs(rho))``eta = (1 - eps_eta) * eta_max(rho) * sigmoid(u_eta)`where `eps_rho` and `eps_eta` are small, documented safety margins and `sigmoid(x) = 1 / (1 + exp(-x))`.Use numerically stable implementations of sigmoid and the inverse transformations.This eta cap is deliberately conservative. Under the specified power-law family and gamma range, it gives a simple sufficient constraint that is stronger than the general SSVI butterfly conditions. Validate the reasoning in the implementation and its tests; do not extend the same bound to another phi family without re-deriving or verifying the relevant conditions.At every decoding step, compute `rho` first, then its associated `eta_max`, then `eta`.Never simulate raw rho, eta, or gamma and merely clip invalid parameters afterwards.### 6.4 ATM term structure constraintsRepresent ATM total variance at the maturity knots as a strictly positive, non-decreasing sequence.One acceptable transformation is:`theta[0] = softplus(a[0]) + theta_floor``theta[j] = theta[j-1] + softplus(a[j]) + increment_floor`for subsequent maturity knots.Use numerically stable `softplus` and its inverse. Choose and document floors that are appropriate for the numeric precision and data.Interpolate theta between maturities using a monotone interpolation scheme or piecewise-linear interpolation. Do not use an unconstrained cubic spline that can introduce decreases between valid knots.Do not extrapolate silently beyond the calibrated maturity range. Define an explicit and tested boundary policy for options outside that range.### 6.5 Required analytical constraint checksFor the selected SSVI model, independently check the applicable Gatheral–Jacquier sufficient conditions, including:`theta * phi(theta) * (1 + abs(rho)) < 4``theta * phi(theta)**2 * (1 + abs(rho)) <= 4`and the calendar conditions for theta and `theta * phi(theta)`.Verify these conditions over the full intended theta domain, not merely at the current option maturities.For this restricted functional family, provide an analytical argument or a reliable bound covering the positive theta domain. Supplement that with numerical checks over a wide logarithmic grid, for example from `1e-10` to `1e4`.Where a general SSVI condition is only sufficient, label it accordingly. Do not interpret failure of a sufficient condition as proof that a surface is arbitrageable.Add a safety margin and explicitly check strict versus non-strict inequalities.If the constrained surface fits the market poorly, report the calibration error. Do not silently relax the no-arbitrage constraints to improve the fit.## 7.
